What is a Data Center?

As the need for more digital storage increases, so do data centers. A data center is a physical room, building, or facility that houses the IT infrastructure for anything related to data and data storage, as well as building, running, or delivering applications and services.1 Data centers have both negative and positive impacts on our planet. They are a practical solution to accomodating IT infrastructure, but it often seems as though the cons outweigh the pros, especially when considering the health of our planet.

Data Centers: the Pros

  • Optimal Resource Utilization | Through virtualization, businesses or clouds can maximize their resources and offer the greatest number of users with the least amount of hardware and idle or unused capacity.1
  • Rapid Service Deployment | Delivering new infrastructure is as simple as submitting a request through a self-service portal by using software-defined infrastructure automation.1
  • Scalability | Compared to traditional IT infrastructure, virtualized IT infrastructure is much simpler to grow; by shifting workloads to the cloud when needed, even businesses using on-premises data centers can add capacity whenever.1

Data Centers: the Cons

  • Energy Expenditure | Data center electricity consumption was estimated to amount around 415 terawatt hours (TWh), or over 1.5% of the world's total electricity consumption in 2024.3
  • Water Consumption | To stop processor chips from overheating, data centers require water for cooling, which threatens our freshwater supply. Large data centers can consume up to 5 million gallons per day; this is equivalent to the water use of a town populated by 10,000 to 50,000 people.2 As data center construction expands and continues, so does their need for water.
  • Data's Demand | Serverboards, circuitry, heat sinks, semiconductors, and microchips are at the heart of these facilities' infrastructures, requiring invaluable minerals that are able to efficiently conduct electricity, corrosion-resistant, and highly conductive.4
  • Artifical Intelligence | Data center expansion is being fueled by the current rise of AI-driven workloads, and by 2030, data center power usage is expected to increase by 160%, according to Goldman Sachs Research.1

Solution

Although several solutions exist, I believe it is important to do our part as residents and custodians of this planet. I propose a combination of several solutions: monitoring AI usage, slowing down the construction of data centers, and, when building data centers, being mindful of the Earth by drafting policies and regulations that are eco-forward. I believe the search for renewable energy solutions to power hyperscale data centers should be at the forefront of our minds. I think all data centers should be sustainable and use energy-efficient technologies to optimize energy use and minimize environmental impact. Organizations may maximize energy use, cut waste, and save money by implementing technologies like virtualization, energy-efficient technology, and renewable energy sources in data centers.1 Furthermore, certifications are essential for identifying and advancing sustainable practices; the Green Grid, Energy Star, and Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design (LEED) are notable associations and certifications.1
